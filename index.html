Research Highlights

Not Every Mention Is a Claim: Rethinking Object Hallucination Detection

Texas A&M FLAIR Lab · Under review at ARR · [paper] [data]

Hallucination benchmarks treat every object a caption names as a claim that the object is present. But in “a bench for people to sit on”, people is mentioned, not asserted. I built NAME-Bench, 25,497 object mentions from five VLMs, labeled as concrete, hallucinated, or non-assertive (depictive, purpose, negation, hedged). Every detector we tested flags non-assertive mentions as hallucinations (14–80%), and correcting this is far harder on newer VLMs than on LLaVA-1.5.

MedAction: Active Multi-turn Clinical Diagnostic LLMs

University of Michigan BioMed-AI Lab · COLM 2026 · [paper] [code/data]

Real diagnosis is a sequence of decisions: what to ask, which test to order, and when to stop. I built a tree-structured distillation pipeline with two knowledge-graph-grounded consistency metrics to create MedAction-32K (32,681 multi-turn diagnostic trajectories from 2,896 PMC cases). Our fine-tuned 8B model is state of the art among open-source models on MedRBench. I am now extending it with reinforcement learning into an agent that orders tests, interprets results, and decides when to stop.

MedPlan: Personalized Medical Plan Generation in Clinical Practice

Far Eastern Memorial Hospital · ACL 2025 Industry Track · [paper] [patent]

A two-stage retrieval-augmented system that mirrors how clinicians write SOAP notes: it first generates an assessment, then a patient-specific treatment plan, grounded in the hospital's real-world EHR records. The system is being piloted at the hospital and validated by attending physicians on real patient records (90%+ ICD-10 prediction accuracy), and led to a Taiwan patent, the National Innovation Award, and MOUs with clinics and HIS vendors. We are now integrating it with HL7 FHIR for interoperability with hospital information systems.

WARP & FORGE: Verifiable Repair of Language Models

NCCU, with Prof. Fang Yu · NSTC Undergraduate Research Grant · [WARP paper] [WARP code] [FORGE paper] [FORGE code]

Fine-tuning can fix a model's mistakes but offers no guarantee about what else it breaks. WARP repairs inner layers of Transformer models by solving a convex quadratic program, with provable guarantees on the repaired samples, preservation of correct behavior, and certified robustness. FORGE extends this to generative LLMs, reducing bias and toxicity with minimal perplexity cost, and stays stable in low-resource settings where fine-tuning fails.
